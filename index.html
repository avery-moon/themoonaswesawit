01 / METHOD

What counts as seeing?

A crescent rendered in gold, a record of an eclipse, a manuscript table, a telescopic drawing and a spacecraft photograph are not interchangeable. This exhibit makes those differences visible. Surface observations can be compared against a modern reference; symbolic, calendrical and mathematical objects are displayed separately.

Our timeline is selective, not a claim to be the first or complete global history of lunar knowledge. Undated living traditions are positioned for navigation only, not assigned an invented origin year. Source links lead to the museums, archives and scientific institutions documenting each entry.

Image note: The central lunar disk and historic-style comparison textures in this prototype are schematic illustrations, not archival reproductions. We will replace them with rights-checked authentic imagery before treating this as a published historical image collection.

02 / SOURCES

Follow the evidence.

Every entry links to its institutional or scholarly source. This is a curated starting collection; candidates with uncertain image provenance or indirect lunar relevance are clearly identified.

Research approach: globally inclusive, artifact-specific, attentive to community-authored knowledge and explicit about uncertainty. AI assistance supports discovery and prototyping; historical interpretation and publication require source review.
